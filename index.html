The conversation before the conversion

Your customer is already having a conversation in their head.

Before they call, book, buy or even follow, they're deciding whether they need this, whether they trust you, whether it's worth the money and what happens if they choose wrong. Good marketing doesn't talk over those questions. It answers them. That's why I don't start with “What should we post this month?” I start with the person we're trying to move.

How I work

Before I decide what your marketing should say,
I figure out what it needs to solve.

01

What's not working?

Where are you losing momentum? Maybe awareness is low. Maybe people are watching but not converting. Maybe a specific service isn't selling or your message sounds like everyone else's. We identify the business problem first.

02

Who are we trying to move?

I research beyond demographics. What are they searching for? What matters to them? What are they afraid of getting wrong? What have they already tried? What makes them skeptical?

03

What's standing between them and yes?

I look for friction: confusion, weak differentiation, missing proof, lack of trust, too many choices or a value proposition people don't immediately understand.

04

What does the marketing need to change?

Attention? Perception? Trust? Understanding? Demand? Action? We give the strategy a job before we give the business more content.

05

Then we build.

Messaging, positioning, content direction, campaigns and social strategy are built around what we learned, not whatever happens to be trending that week.

06

Then behavior becomes information.

I watch what people view, ignore, share, question, click and respond to. That behavior tells us what to improve next.

Behavioral science in the work

Marketing is asking someone to make a decision. I want to understand what's influencing that decision.

People don't experience your marketing as a spreadsheet, funnel or content calendar. They experience attention, confusion, curiosity, risk, familiarity, trust, skepticism and desire. Behavioral science gives me a sharper way to question what's happening before someone acts.

AttentionWhat makes someone notice this in the first place, and what gets ignored?
Cognitive loadAre we making the customer work too hard to understand what we're saying?
Decision frictionWhat is making the next step feel difficult, uncertain or risky?
TrustWhat evidence would make this claim easier to believe?
Social proofWhat can other people's experiences communicate that the brand can't credibly say about itself?
PerceptionWhat are the words, visuals and context making someone assume before we've explicitly told them anything?

I use those questions to make better marketing decisions. The lab is a small demonstration of how context can change perception.
